How to choose the right wood burning kit

Updated June 2026Independently researchedNo paid placement.

Quick answer

For most beginners, the best wood burning kit is a temperature-controlled pen with a variety of interchangeable tips, a sturdy stand, and a starter set of practice boards, look for kits that include safety accessories and clear instructions. Prioritize adjustable heat and tip variety over extra accessories you won’t use, and expect to spend anywhere from budget-friendly to a few hundred dollars depending on features and brand.

What should a beginner look for in a wood burning kit?

If you’re just starting out, focus on three things: temperature control, tip variety, and safety. A variable-temperature pen lets you adjust heat for different woods and line weights, fixed-temperature pens are harder to control and can scorch soft woods. Look for a kit that includes at least a few basic tips like a universal point, a shading tip, and a calligraphy edge, plus a stand to hold the hot pen safely. Also check that the kit includes a decent selection of practice boards, smooth, light-colored woods like basswood or birch are ideal for beginners. Many kits come with a handful of small boards, but you’ll likely want to buy extra separately. Finally, make sure the kit includes a heat-resistant work surface or at least a metal stand, and consider buying a pair of heat-resistant gloves if they’re not included.

Variable temperature vs. fixed temperature: which is better?

Variable-temperature pens are the clear winner for anyone who plans to do more than a single project. They let you dial in the exact heat needed for fine lines, deep shading, or burning on different wood types. Fixed-temperature pens are simpler and often cheaper, but they run hot and can be frustrating for detailed work, you’ll have less control over line thickness and shading. If you’re on a tight budget, a fixed-temperature pen can still work for basic outlines, but you’ll quickly hit its limits. Most experienced burners recommend starting with a variable-temperature kit, even if it costs a bit more. Look for a pen with a comfortable grip and a cord that doesn’t get in the way, some kits offer cordless options, but they tend to have shorter battery life and may not heat as consistently.

What types of wood burning tips are essential?

A good starter kit should include at least five or six tips, but you don’t need a huge set. The essentials are a universal point for fine lines, a shading tip (often a flat or spoon-shaped tip) for smooth gradients, and a calligraphy edge for bold, wide strokes. A stippling tip is also handy for texture, and a razor or knife tip can help with detail work. Avoid kits that include dozens of obscure tips you’ll never use, they often sacrifice quality for quantity. Instead, look for a kit that offers a few well-made tips and the option to buy additional ones separately. Also check that the tips are made of brass or a similar durable material, and that they fit securely in the pen without wobbling.

How do I choose the right wood for burning?

Soft, light-colored woods are easiest for beginners because they burn evenly and show contrast well. Basswood, pine, and birch are popular choices, basswood is especially smooth and forgiving. Avoid woods with heavy grain or resin, like cedar or oak, which can burn unevenly and produce more smoke. Your kit may include practice boards, but you’ll eventually want to buy your own. Look for pre-cut panels or craft wood that’s sanded smooth, rough surfaces make it hard to control the pen. You can also experiment with different woods to see which you like, but stick to untreated, unfinished wood; any varnish or paint will release harmful fumes when burned.

What safety gear should come with a wood burning kit?

At minimum, a kit should include a heat-resistant stand for the pen, and ideally a pair of heat-resistant gloves. A work surface that can withstand heat is also important, many kits include a small metal or ceramic plate, but you can also use a piece of tile or a dedicated craft mat. Always work in a well-ventilated area, as burning wood produces smoke and fumes. Some kits include a carbon filter or a small fan to help with fumes, but these are not essential for beginners. More important is to have a dedicated space where you can leave the hot pen to cool down safely. Never leave a burning pen unattended, and keep a fire extinguisher or a bowl of water nearby just in case.

How much should I expect to spend on a quality wood burning kit?

Prices vary widely, but you can find a decent beginner kit for a budget-friendly amount, and a more advanced kit with multiple pens and accessories for a few hundred dollars. The sweet spot for most hobbyists is a mid-range kit that includes a variable-temperature pen, a handful of tips, a stand, and a few practice boards, this will give you room to grow without breaking the bank. Be wary of extremely cheap kits that may have poor temperature control or flimsy tips. On the other hand, you don’t need the most expensive professional setup unless you’re planning to sell your work or do it daily. Read owner reviews to see how durable the pen is and whether the temperature dial is accurate, those are the two most common complaints.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a wood burning kit on any type of wood?

No, soft, light woods like basswood, pine, and birch are best. Hardwoods and woods with heavy grain, like oak or walnut, are harder to burn and may produce uneven results. Always use untreated, unfinished wood to avoid toxic fumes.

How long does it take to learn wood burning?

Most beginners can learn basic outlining and shading within a few hours of practice, but mastering detailed portraits or lettering takes weeks or months. Start with simple patterns and practice on scrap wood to build confidence.

Are cordless wood burning pens as good as corded ones?

Cordless pens offer more freedom of movement, but they often have shorter battery life and may not maintain consistent heat as the battery drains. For long sessions, a corded pen with a comfortable cord is usually more reliable.

What’s the difference between wood burning and pyrography?

They’re the same thing, pyrography is the formal term for the art of decorating wood with burn marks. A wood burning kit is the tool used for pyrography, and the two terms are often used interchangeably.

Do I need to sand wood before burning?

Yes, sanding smooths the surface and removes any rough spots that can catch the pen. Most practice boards come pre-sanded, but if you’re using your own wood, sand with fine-grit paper (around 220 grit) before you start.

Can I use stencils with a wood burning kit?

Absolutely, stencils are a great way to transfer designs onto wood. You can trace the design with carbon paper, or use a stencil and a fine-tip pen to outline the pattern. Just be sure to let the pen cool slightly before tracing to avoid burning through the stencil.

In shortChoosing the right wood burning kit comes down to matching the tool to your skill level and goals. Start with a variable-temperature pen, a few essential tips, and a safe workspace, then practice on soft woods until you find your rhythm. With a little patience, you’ll be creating beautiful, lasting pieces in no time.
